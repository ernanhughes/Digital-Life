WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

CONNECTED GEOMETRY

Occupied cells form one connected shape. The chapter tests whether that visible unity deserves causal privilege.

PRIVILEGED BOUNDARY

A boundary that the dynamics themselves treat as special. Drawing a circle does not make it privileged.

SELF-PREDICTION GAIN

How much a region's own present state improves prediction of its future beyond what surrounding state already predicts.

EXCESS PREDICTIVE COHERENCE

The real self-prediction gain minus a spatially scrambled null. It asks whether prediction is stronger than a control version.

FAMILY-LEVEL NULL

A control that accounts for searching across several candidate scales. It asks whether the best-looking result is unusual after the search itself is included.

CAUSAL LOCALIZATION

A perturbation has stronger effects on the same side of a boundary than across it. This can happen at arbitrary cuts in a local system.

SAME-SIDE LOCALIZATION

Inside perturbations mostly affect inside, and outside perturbations mostly affect outside. It shows locality, not individuality.

BULK VS FLUX

Bulk is what remains occupied. Flux is where material transitions are happening. The causal process may be better seen in the flux than in the snapshot.

PROCESS-FIRST METHOD

Measure causal activity first, then ask whether a boundary emerges. Do not draw the body first and try to justify it afterward.
