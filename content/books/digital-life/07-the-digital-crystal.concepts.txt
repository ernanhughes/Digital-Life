WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

DIGITAL CRYSTAL

A local computational growth process whose final shape can carry information about conditions during formation. The name is earned only in this narrow sense.

HEXAGONAL LATTICE

A grid where each location has six immediate neighbours. It makes local attachment geometry simple and symmetric.

IRREVERSIBLE OCCUPANCY

Once a cell becomes occupied, it stays occupied. This is an assumption of the early crystal, not a law of digital systems.

STOCHASTIC ATTACHMENT

Attachment happens with a probability rather than certainty. This gives the environment a way to influence growth.

ENVIRONMENTAL FORCING

An external scalar value that changes attachment conditions at each step. It influences growth but does not draw the final shape.

ANISOTROPY

A built-in directional bias. Growth is not equally likely in every direction.

CROWDING PENALTY

A reduction in attachment probability when a candidate has too many occupied neighbours. It prevents crowded sites from gaining unlimited advantage.

SOURCE-FAMILY RECOVERY

Using final morphology to identify which kind of environmental process produced it. This worked above chance for broad source families.

TEMPORAL ORGANIZATION

The order of the same values through time. The chapter did not establish that final morphology could recover this ordering.

LOSSY INTEGRATION

The process preserves broad consequences of formation conditions while losing detailed chronology.
