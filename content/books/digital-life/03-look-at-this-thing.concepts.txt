WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

CELLULAR AUTOMATON

A world made of many locations that update by the same local rule. Each location changes based on nearby values, not because a central controller tells it what to do.

LENIA

A continuous cellular automaton whose patterns can look creature-like. "Continuous" means locations can hold graded values rather than only 0 or 1.

LOCAL DYNAMICS

Rules that act only on nearby state. Large patterns can appear even when every update is local and simple.

LOCALIZED ORGANIZATION

A pattern that stays concentrated in one region instead of spreading everywhere. It may look like an object, but that must be tested.

PROPAGATION

Something recognizable reappears in new places without the same material moving there.

```
state here
v influences nearby updates
similar organization appears over there
```

MATERIAL IDENTITY

The same parts persist through time. A moving glider in the Game of Life does not preserve the same active cells.

ORGANIZATIONAL IDENTITY

A recognizable arrangement persists even though the parts change. The glider keeps its organization, not its material.

HISTORY DEPENDENCE

Something earlier changes what happens later. In the Physarum-inspired model, earlier traces and agent positions affect later network formation.

STIGMERGY

Coordination through changes left in a shared environment. Agents alter the field, and the altered field changes later agent behavior.

ROUTE IDENTITY VS ORGANIZATIONAL CONTINUITY

Route identity asks whether the same historical paths remain. Organizational continuity asks whether the same broad kind of structure keeps being maintained.
