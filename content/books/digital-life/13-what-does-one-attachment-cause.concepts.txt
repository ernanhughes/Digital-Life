WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

LOCAL-RULE EFFECT

The immediate effect predicted directly by the attachment rule. If one cell attaches, neighbouring candidate probabilities change.

FORCE/PREVENT INTERVENTION

Two branches from the same checkpoint: one forces a candidate cell to attach, the other prevents it. The intervention cell itself is excluded from measured outcomes.

MECHANICALLY EXPECTED GAIN

The one-step neighbouring construction difference predicted by probabilities before random draws. It is the rule's own expected effect.

TRANSIENT ARM

A branch where the forced cell gets one causal exposure and is then removed. This tests whether downstream consequences continue after the initiating difference is gone.

RETAINED ARM

A branch where the forced cell is not experimentally removed. Its later fate follows ordinary loss and reoccupation.

RETAINED STATE DIFFERENCE VS CASCADE

If the initiating cell remains, later effects may be due to its continuing presence rather than a self-sustaining cascade.

FRONTIER CREATION POTENTIAL

How much occupying a candidate changes the number of frontier opportunities. It is observer-defined and did not become a stable predictor here.

ESTIMATOR MANUFACTURE

A statistic can create the appearance of the pattern it was built to summarize. The failed propagation estimator produced a fake ridge from noise and one anchored row.

FAR-FIELD DIFFERENCE

A construction difference outside the local measurement region. The chapter measures it but does not yet identify its mechanism.
