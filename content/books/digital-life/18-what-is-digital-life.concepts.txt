WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

PROCESS-FIRST VIEW

Start by studying continuing causal processes, not by assuming an organism or boundary must come first.

ORGANIZED CAUSAL CONSEQUENCE

Earlier states create consequences that remain relevant to later possibilities in an organized way. This is proposed as a research primitive, not a definition of life.

MATERIAL IDENTITY VS PROCESS CONTINUITY

The same material tokens need not persist for a process to continue. Turnover can happen while causal organization continues.

ACTIVE INTERFACE

The dynamically generated locations where transitions are currently possible. It is not the same as a visible body boundary.

FINITE COMPUTATIONAL OPPORTUNITY

Only some eligible transitions receive evaluation. This is a computational scarcity, not metabolism.

HIDDEN-STATE CAUSALITY

Hidden state changes future response without needing to be readable as a record. This is narrower than memory.

CAUSAL ORGANIZATION BEFORE INDIVIDUALITY

The book measured causal structure before establishing any privileged individual boundary. That is a methodological result, not a proof that individuals are unnecessary.

SUBSTRATE AFFORDANCE VS EXPERIMENTAL FINDING

An affordance is what computation makes possible, such as checkpointing. A finding is what this process was measured to do, such as loss creating construction opportunities.

NEGATIVE SPECIFICATION

A list of things that are not enough by themselves: motion is not life, refilling is not repair, containment is not individuality, and so on.

ORGANIZATIONAL SELF-CONDITIONING

A speculative missing dimension: the process's organization helps maintain or recreate the conditions of its own continuation.
